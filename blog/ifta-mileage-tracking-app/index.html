IFTA mileage tracking apps for owner-operators and hot shots

November 25, 2026 · By David Kulagin, maker of FuelPilot

Your IFTA return needs your miles in every state and the fuel you bought in each. If you run an ELD, check whether it already reports miles by state. If you don't, a phone app can do it: GPS apps like IFTA Track log state miles as you drive, manual apps like IFTA Ledger do the quarterly math from what you enter, and FuelPilot works out state miles from the trips you navigate and the receipts you scan, and also plans your fuel stops.

I make FuelPilot, so weigh that. This isn't tax advice. Your base state's IFTA office has the final word on what your records need to show.

Do you need IFTA?

You need an IFTA license if you run a qualified motor vehicle in more than one IFTA state or province. Under the agreement, a qualified motor vehicle carries people or property and:

  • has two axles and a gross or registered gross weight over 26,000 lb, or
  • has three or more axles, regardless of weight, or
  • is used in combination with a total weight over 26,000 lb.

That last line is the one that catches hot shots. A one-ton pickup and gooseneck rated over 26,000 lb together qualify, even though the pickup alone doesn't. Recreational vehicles are not qualified motor vehicles.

When returns are due

Returns are filed quarterly with your base state, by the last day of the month after each quarter:

  • January to March: April 30
  • April to June: July 31
  • July to September: October 31
  • October to December: January 31

What your records need

For each quarter you report total miles and the miles in each state, and the fuel you bought in each state. IFTA licensees have to keep records that back up every return, and you can be audited. In practice that means trip records showing where you drove and your fuel receipts. Ask your base state what it accepts as the source of your miles before you rely on an app alone.

Your ELD

If you already run an ELD, look for an IFTA or state mileage report in it first. It's the least extra work. If you're exempt from the ELD rule, as some short-haul operations are, you'll need another way.

FuelPilot

Every trip you navigate with FuelPilot is logged, and miles by state are worked out from the GPS path, checked against official state boundaries. Photograph a fuel receipt and the station, gallons and price are read for you, and the state comes from where it was scanned. With Pro, export a quarterly IFTA summary with miles and gallons by state as a spreadsheet.

It also does the thing the IFTA apps don't: Autopilot picks the cheapest fuel stop you can reach for every stop on the run. With Big Rig, routes are truck-legal for your rig's height, weight and length, and fuel stops are truck stops and truck lanes. There's a Hot shot / HD pickup vehicle type.

The catch: drives you don't navigate with FuelPilot aren't included. Navigate every loaded and empty leg, and check the totals before you file.

IFTA Track

IFTA Track uses your phone's GPS to log miles by state or province automatically, and builds summary reports with miles and taxable gallons by jurisdiction. It's free to download with in-app purchases. It needs an internet connection and runs location in the background, which uses battery.

IFTA Ledger

If you'd rather enter numbers yourself, IFTA Ledger is a trip log and IFTA calculator that works offline. You log miles and fuel by state and it builds the quarterly summary. Everything unlocks for a one-time $14.99.

Paper and odometer

Write down the odometer at every state line, plus each fuel purchase. It's free and auditors know it, but it's easy to miss a line and slow to total up every quarter.

Our take

  • You have an ELD with IFTA reports: use it, and keep your fuel receipts.
  • You want to set it and forget it: a background GPS app.
  • You want state miles and cheaper fuel from one app: FuelPilot, as long as you navigate every leg with it.

Questions

Does a hot shot truck need IFTA?

If the truck and trailer together weigh or are registered over 26,000 lb and you run in more than one IFTA state or province, yes. The combination rule counts the trailer, so a pickup that doesn't qualify alone can qualify with a gooseneck behind it.

When are IFTA returns due?

Quarterly, by the last day of the month after the quarter ends: April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31.

Can I use a phone app to track IFTA miles?

Yes. Apps can log miles by state from your phone's GPS or work them out from your routes. Keep your fuel receipts and the trip records behind the totals, and check with your base state what records it expects in an audit.

Does FuelPilot do IFTA?

Yes, with FuelPilot Pro or Big Rig. It works out miles by state from the GPS path of the trips you navigate and gallons by state from your scanned receipts, and exports a quarterly summary as a spreadsheet. Drives you don't navigate with FuelPilot aren't included.

Do RVs need IFTA?

No. Recreational vehicles are not qualified motor vehicles under IFTA.
