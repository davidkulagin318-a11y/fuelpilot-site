How it's worked out

  • Towing MPG = your towing MPG, or MPG × (1 − drop ÷ 100)
  • Full-tank range = usable tank × towing MPG
  • Miles between stops = full-tank range × (1 − reserve ÷ 100)

Example: a 36-gallon tank at 9 MPG towing is 324 miles on a full tank. Keep a quarter tank in reserve and you've got about 243 miles between stops.

What drop to use

When Oak Ridge National Laboratory tested an SUV towing a 3,500 lb enclosed cargo trailer for the Department of Energy, fuel economy fell by about 30% in city driving and about 50% at 80 mph. Heavier, taller trailers, hills, headwinds and higher speeds push it toward the top of that range. The 35% starting value is a middle guess. Your own fill-ups are the only real answer: divide miles towed by gallons to refill, over two or three tanks, and put that in the towing MPG box.

For tank size, use the gallons it takes to fill up from empty, not a guess. Fuel gauges aren't exact, which is one more reason to keep a reserve.

Plan stops around your real range

FuelPilot's towing mode adjusts your range for the trailer and learns your real MPG from your fill-ups. Autopilot then picks the cheapest stations you can reach for the whole trip, keeping the reserve you choose, and adds them to your turn-by-turn directions. Questions

How far can I tow on a tank of gas or diesel?

Multiply your usable tank size by your towing MPG. A 36-gallon tank at 9 MPG gives 324 miles on a full tank. Keeping a quarter tank in reserve leaves about 243 miles between stops.

How much does towing reduce range?

About as much as it reduces MPG. In a Department of Energy test, an SUV towing a 3,500 lb enclosed trailer lost about 30% of its fuel economy in city driving and about 50% at 80 mph. Heavy travel trailers and fifth wheels often fall somewhere in that range, depending on speed, wind and hills.

How much reserve should I keep when towing?

A quarter tank is a common rule. Keep more in remote areas, in winter, or before a long climb.

Why is my towing range lower than the calculator says?

Headwinds, hills, speed and idling all cut MPG, and fuel gauges aren't exact. Measure your towing MPG over a few tanks and use that.
