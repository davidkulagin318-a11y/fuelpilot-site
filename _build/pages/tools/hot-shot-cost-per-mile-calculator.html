The diesel price starts at the US average from the EIA, ${diesel_price} ({diesel_date}). Change it to what you pay. A month is counted as 52 ÷ 12 weeks. Nothing you enter leaves your browser.

How it's worked out

  • Miles per month = miles per week × 52 ÷ 12
  • Fuel per mile = diesel price ÷ MPG
  • Monthly costs per mile = monthly costs ÷ miles per month
  • Total cost per mile = fuel + maintenance and tires + monthly costs per mile
  • Break-even rate per loaded mile = total cost per mile ÷ (1 − empty miles ÷ 100)

The last step matters most. You pay for every mile, but you're only paid for the loaded ones. With 15% of your miles empty, every loaded mile has to cover about 1.18 miles of cost.

Example, using the numbers filled in above with diesel at $5.94: fuel is about 59¢ a mile, running costs about 74¢, and $2,500 a month in fixed costs over about 10,833 miles adds about 23¢. That's about 97¢ for every mile driven, or about $1.15 per loaded mile just to break even, before you pay yourself.

What's not included

  • Taxes. Income and self-employment tax, and any fuel tax you settle through IFTA, depend on your situation.
  • Tolls, scales and lumpers. Add a monthly average under Everything else.
  • Dispatch or factoring fees. If you pay a percentage, your rate has to cover that on top.

Questions

How do I calculate cost per mile for a hot shot truck?

Add up your fuel per mile (diesel price ÷ MPG), maintenance and tires per mile, and your monthly fixed costs divided by the miles you drive in a month. Then divide by the share of miles that are loaded to get the rate you need per loaded mile.

What's a good cost per mile for hot shot trucking?

There isn't one number. It depends on your truck, your payments, insurance, MPG and how many miles you run. Running more miles spreads your fixed costs thinner, so the same truck costs less per mile the more it runs. Work out your own with the calculator and keep it current as fuel prices change.

How do empty miles affect my rate?

You pay for empty miles but aren't paid for them, so their cost has to come out of your loaded miles. Divide your cost per mile by the share of miles that are loaded. At 15% empty, multiply by about 1.18.

Does it work for a semi?

Yes. The math is the same for any truck. Put in your own MPG, miles and monthly costs.
