Mileage tracker apps for work: FuelPilot vs MileIQ vs Everlance

November 25, 2026 · By David Kulagin, maker of FuelPilot

MileIQ and Everlance log your drives automatically in the background, whether or not you open them. FuelPilot logs the drives you navigate with it, and also plans your fuel stops and tracks fuel expenses. If you want every drive caught without thinking about it, pick MileIQ or Everlance. If most of your work driving is trips you'd use directions for anyway, and fuel is a big cost, FuelPilot does both jobs.

I make FuelPilot, so weigh that. Prices are US prices from each company's site in November 2026.

Why it matters

For 2026, the IRS standard mileage rate for business use is 72.5 cents a mile. At that rate, 10,000 business miles is a $7,250 deduction, so missed trips cost real money. Whatever app you use, the log has to be one you'll actually keep up. Ask your tax preparer what your records need to show.

MileIQ

Best for: anyone who wants every drive logged without touching the app.

MileIQ runs in the background and detects drives on its own. You swipe each one business or personal and it builds the reports. The free plan covers 40 drives a month; Unlimited is $7.50 a month billed yearly or $8.99 month to month.

Everlance

Best for: self-employed drivers who want mileage and other business expenses in one place.

Everlance also detects trips automatically. The free Basic plan includes 30 automatic trips a month and unlimited manual trips. Starter, with unlimited automatic tracking, is $8.99 a month or $69.99 a year, and a Professional plan is $99.99 a year.

FuelPilot

Best for: drivers who navigate to their jobs and spend a lot on fuel.

  • Every drive you navigate with FuelPilot is saved with its route, miles, time and fuel. Tag it business or personal and add a note, like the client or job.
  • Photograph a fuel receipt and, with Pro, the station, gallons, price and total are read for you.
  • Export a mileage log, a fuel expense report or a quarterly IFTA summary as a spreadsheet.
  • Autopilot plans each fuel stop at the cheapest station you can reach, so the drive costs less in the first place.

The catch: FuelPilot doesn't detect drives on its own. A drive you make without starting directions in the app isn't logged. If you often hop in the truck without navigating, you'll miss trips.

Which one to pick

  • Lots of short local drives, no navigation: MileIQ or Everlance.
  • You want receipts and other expenses too: Everlance.
  • Long work drives you navigate anyway, a diesel truck, or IFTA: FuelPilot. One yearly Pro plan covers the log, the fuel stops and the exports.
  • Both: some drivers use a background tracker for the log and FuelPilot for the fuel planning.

Questions

What is the IRS mileage rate for 2026?

The IRS standard mileage rate for business use of a car, van, pickup or panel truck is 72.5 cents a mile for 2026, up 2.5 cents from 2025.

Does FuelPilot track mileage automatically like MileIQ?

Not in the same way. MileIQ detects drives in the background. FuelPilot logs every drive you navigate with it, with no extra taps, but doesn't record drives where you didn't start directions in the app.

Which mileage app is cheapest?

Each has a free plan: MileIQ covers 40 drives a month, Everlance 30 automatic trips a month plus manual trips, and FuelPilot your last 7 days of trips. On paid plans, FuelPilot Pro is $49.99 a year, Everlance Starter $69.99 a year and MileIQ Unlimited $7.50 a month billed yearly.

Is there a mileage tracker that also saves money on gas?

FuelPilot logs the drives you navigate and plans each fuel stop at the cheapest station you can reach on your route.

Which app works for IFTA?

Of these three, only FuelPilot exports a quarterly IFTA summary with miles and gallons by state. See IFTA mileage tracking apps for owner-operators.
