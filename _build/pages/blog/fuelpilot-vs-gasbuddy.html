FuelPilot vs GasBuddy: a price map or a planned route?

November 25, 2026 · By David Kulagin, maker of FuelPilot

GasBuddy tells you what fuel costs near you and saves you money at the pump with its payment card. FuelPilot is turn-by-turn navigation that plans your fuel stops for the whole trip from your tank and your real MPG. For cheap gas around town, GasBuddy is hard to beat. For long trips, towing and diesel, FuelPilot does the planning GasBuddy leaves to you.

I make FuelPilot, so weigh this with that in mind. I've tried to be straight about where GasBuddy is the better app.

What GasBuddy does well

  • Price coverage. GasBuddy's users report prices as they fill up, so in most towns you can see what every station is charging right now.
  • Money off at the pump. Pay with GasBuddy is a payment card that takes money off each gallon. The paid Plus and Premium plans save 20¢ a gallon on your first 50 gallons each month, and Premium adds roadside assistance.
  • Free tools. The app is free to use, with a trip cost calculator, a fuel log, fuel outage tracking and vehicle recall alerts.
  • Route search. You can see prices along a route and choose where to stop.

If you mostly drive around town and want the cheapest gas nearby, GasBuddy does that job well and for free.

What FuelPilot does differently

A price map tells you where fuel is cheap. It doesn't know how far your tank will get you, so you still have to work out which of those stations you can reach, then switch to a maps app to get there. On a long trip, or with a trailer behind you, that's where people run low or overpay at the only station left.

  • It plans from your range. FuelPilot knows your tank size, learns your real MPG from your fill-ups and works out how far you can go.
  • Autopilot picks the stops. Before you leave, it picks the cheapest stations you can reach for the whole trip, keeping the reserve you choose, and adds them to your directions. After each fill-up it checks that the next stop still makes sense.
  • Towing mode. Turn it on when you hitch up and your range is planned for the trailer.
  • It's the navigation app. Turn-by-turn directions with spoken guidance are built in, so you don't switch apps.
  • Trips log themselves. Every drive you navigate is saved with its miles, time and fuel. With Pro you can export a mileage log, a fuel expense report or a quarterly IFTA summary.
  • Trucks and RVs. The Big Rig plan routes for your rig's height, weight and length and sends semis and hot shots to truck stops and truck lanes only.

Where GasBuddy is the better pick

  • You drive locally and just want the cheapest gas near you.
  • You want a discount card at the pump. FuelPilot doesn't have one.
  • You want crowd-reported prices. FuelPilot's prices come from HERE and can lag behind the pump, so the price on the pump is the one you pay.
  • You don't want to pay anything. FuelPilot's free plan has navigation and the cheapest gas near you, but Autopilot needs Pro.

Where FuelPilot is the better pick

  • Road trips and long runs where the stop you choose depends on how far your tank goes.
  • Towing a travel trailer, fifth wheel or cargo trailer, when your range drops and you need stations you can actually reach.
  • Diesel pickups, hot shots, semis and RVs that need truck-legal routes or truck lanes.
  • Driving for work, when you need a mileage log or fuel expense report at tax time.

Can you use both?

Yes. If you pay with a discount card, enter the discount once in FuelPilot under Settings › My discounts and it compares stations on the price you actually pay. Plenty of drivers keep GasBuddy for local fill-ups and plan road trips in FuelPilot.

Questions

Is FuelPilot better than GasBuddy?

They do different jobs. GasBuddy is better for finding cheap gas around town and for its pump discount card. FuelPilot is better for trips: it's a navigation app that picks the cheapest fuel stops you can reach from your tank and real MPG and adds them to your route.

Does GasBuddy plan fuel stops for a road trip?

GasBuddy shows prices along a route so you can choose where to stop. FuelPilot's Autopilot picks the stops for you from your range and reserve and adds them to your turn-by-turn directions.

Is FuelPilot free like GasBuddy?

FuelPilot is free to download and use for navigation and the cheapest gas near you, and a free account shows fuel prices along your route. Autopilot, towing mode and exports come with FuelPilot Pro, $7.99 a month or $49.99 a year, with a 7-day free trial.

Where do FuelPilot's fuel prices come from?

From HERE Technologies. GasBuddy's prices are reported by its users. Any app's prices can lag behind the pump, so the price on the pump is the one you pay.

Do both apps work in Canada?

Yes. Both work in the United States and Canada.
